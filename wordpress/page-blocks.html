Where can a circuit rider, water technician, district WASH officer or health facility cleaner go to learn? To find out, the Water Institute at UNC catalogued publicly available WASH trainings and the webinars shared on sector mailing lists between October 2025 and October 2026, and coded each training for who it is designed for.

Circuit riders, and the operators and local officers they support, keep rural water systems running, but they are not who most training is built for. Most free courses that come with a certificate assume a degree-holding NGO or government professional who reads English. We put this together to make the useful courses easier to find and to show where training is still missing.

The directory lists 101 trainings from 94 organizations that were open or running as of October 2026. The webinar library is separate: it lists 61 individual webinars that have a public recording. A few organizations, such as RWSN, appear in both. The directory lists their webinar series as one entry, and the library lists the individual sessions.

How we put this together

  • What we included: publicly advertised WASH trainings, including self-paced e-learning, short courses, MOOCs, certificates, diplomas and degrees, webinar series and open training packages, from UN agencies, humanitarian organizations, NGOs and networks, universities, national training institutes and commercial training companies.
  • How we found them: a starting list of organizations known for WASH training (RWSN, Global Water Center, Emory, UNICEF, WHO, CAWST and others), three rounds of structured web searches including non-English and regional institutes, and webinar announcements from sector mailing lists. We traced recordings on YouTube, Vimeo and organizers’ own sites.
  • How we coded them: for each training we recorded the WASH experience and education it assumes, its primary audience, format, cost, certificate, languages and status, based on what the organization publishes.
  • What we left out: of the 128 trainings we found, 21 looked dormant or had no current dates and 6 had links we could not get to work, so they are not listed here. In-person national courses and organizations’ internal training are under-represented.
  • When: this is a snapshot from September and October 2026. Costs, dates and certificates change often, so check with the organization before you enroll.

Who the trainings are built for

Each row is an audience. Each column is a feature that makes a training easier to take. Numbers are counts of trainings in the directory, and a training can count in more than one row.

Languages other than English: French (28), Spanish (22), Arabic (5), Portuguese (5), German (3), Bengali (1), Filipino (1), Indonesian (1), Nepali (1), Russian (1). Another 18 trainings say they are available in several languages without listing them.

The last row counts trainings coded with operation and maintenance as a topic. It is our best indicator of the skills circuit riders use day to day, not a list of courses designed for circuit riders.

Gaps in training

Six gaps stand out from the scan.

1. Operators and technicians

Only 14 of the 101 trainings name technicians or operators as a primary audience, and just one of those is both free and certificated. Most hands-on operator training is paid, in person and national, such as the Maynilad Water Academy in the Philippines and the Global Water Center’s operator courses.

2. Operation and maintenance

Only 12 trainings cover operation and maintenance. Self-paced material on community-level O&M, preventive maintenance and post-construction support is thin compared with courses on policy, finance and sanitation planning.

3. Learners without a degree

36 trainings are open to people without a college degree, often humanitarian or WHO e-learning, but many assume reading fluency in English and a reliable internet connection.

4. Local languages

About half the trainings (51 of 101) are in English only. French, Spanish and Portuguese options come mostly from WHO, PAHO, the Inter-American Development Bank, Brazil’s Escola Virtual and francophone institutes. Local-language training for frontline staff is rare.

5. Data, monitoring and climate

Monitoring beyond JMP basics, practical data tools for service providers, and climate-resilient WASH are emerging topics with only a handful of dedicated trainings (9 on data and monitoring, 12 on climate). Menstrual health mostly appears as a sub-topic.

6. Continuity and recordings

About one in six of the trainings we found (21 of 128) looked dormant or had no current dates. Webinars hold up better: 61 of the 80 webinars in our archive have a public recording, but they are scattered across YouTube, Vimeo and organizers’ own sites.
